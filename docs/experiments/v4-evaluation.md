# Pogofish v4 — common evaluation of every player

Date: 2026-11-08 · Raw: `v4/` (one JSON per player and opponent, each with its full
command, git revision and table hash) · Bench: `crates/infer/src/bin/v4bench.rs`, players
in `crates/infer/src/players.rs` · Tables below generated by `tools/v4_table.py` · End to
end: `scripts/v4-eval.sh`

Every player built so far, from random to perfect play, measured on one protocol so that
their figures can stand side by side. Earlier rounds measured each player with its own
arena, seed and opponent set; their figures are not comparable with one another or with
these.

## Protocol

One binary, `v4bench`, measures one player two ways. It needs no libtorch: nets are read
from their pure-Rust exports.

**Reference.** The solved uncapped game of `v3-solve.md`, its table fingerprinted below.
Every JSON carries the table's SHA-256 (`table_sha256`); `tools/v4_table.py` refuses (exit 2)
a JSON whose hash differs or that has no `command`.

**Sample.** Round 3's held-out sample (`v3-principles-perfect.md`), drawn in the same order
(`perfect learn`, sample seed 31): 20,000 won and 20,000 drawn training positions (seeds 31
and 32) are drawn first, then 10,000 won and 10,000 drawn held-out positions (seeds 33 and
34), excluding the training ones; "won" means won in 3 plies or more. Only the held-out
positions are measured; the training set is drawn so that the held-out set is round 3's. Each reachable position of
a class weighs the same, so the sample is not made of positions met in play. Two strata, won
and drawn; lost positions are not sampled. (`sample` in each JSON.)

**History-free positions.** Sample positions come from `unrank`: they carry no history
(`"history": "none"` in each JSON). A net with repetition inputs therefore reads every
sample position as never seen before, which is not what it sees in a game. The keep rates
below are measured on such positions; the arena scores are played in real games, with
history.

**Value kept.** Per position, the chance that a uniform pick from the player's *tied set*
(the moves it would choose between at random) keeps the uncapped value: from a won position,
a move to a position lost for the opponent; from a drawn one, a move to a position the
opponent does not win. The chance is computed exactly from the tied set, with no draw. The
rate is the mean over the positions of a class, with a 95 % interval mean ± 1.96·s/√n over
positions (round 3's method).

**Score against greedy.** The arena of round 3 (`v3-principles-perfect.md` § Arena): rules
`lc1-2`, opponent `greedy`, 1,000 pairs, each a random 4-ply opening played twice with
colours swapped, arena seed 404, 1,000-ply limit. The score is the mean over pairs of the
player's two results (win 1, draw ½, loss 0), with a 95 % interval over pairs. A pair with an
unfinished game is left out of the score and counted apart; an unfinished game is never a
draw (`crates/search/src/arena.rs`). Each player picks uniformly from its tied set with the
arena's generator.

**Players** (`players.rs`, module documentation):

- `table`: perfect play of the uncapped game, read from the table. The table ignores
  repetition, so the moves that lose at once under the live rules (a third occurrence under
  `lc1-2`) are set aside first, in every class, unless every candidate does; then from a won
  position it takes the fastest win (the moves to `Loss(d)` with the smallest `d`), from a
  drawn one any move whose child the opponent does not win, from a lost one the slowest loss
  (`Win(d)` with the largest `d`). The rule is recorded in each JSON (`table_tiebreak`).
- `random`: every legal move, after taking a move that wins at once and avoiding one that
  loses at once. This is round 3's `Policy::Random`, the only definition that reproduces its
  held-out rates 0.360 / 0.112. It is not the arena's uniform `Scripted::Random`, which round
  3's arena used for its "random vs greedy" control (0.001, `v3-principles-perfect/arena.json`;
  `crates/search/src/bin/perfect.rs`): the two random rows are different players.
- `greedy`: the moves leaving the most tops after the move (round 3's list `[top_diff up]`,
  which reproduces `Scripted::Greedy` move for move). `greedy-2ply`: the same count, one ply
  deeper (`GreedyTwoPly`).
- `qtab:<v.bin>`: the tabular learner of this round (pre-registration below), one ply deep.
- `td:<pfv>`: a TD(λ) value net, one ply deep: the moves with the best exact result when they
  end the game under the live rules, else the highest −V(position after the move); exact
  float ties only.
- `net:<pfw>:100`: an AlphaZero net's most-visited move(s) after 100 simulations, no root
  noise (round 2's protocol). `net:<pfw>:0`: the move(s) of highest prior, no search.

The AlphaZero nets are `az-lc1-s1`, round 2's seed-1 net at iteration 200 (the terminal
game's and the site's opponent), and `az-lc1-m2-s1` to `-s3`, round 2's configuration rerun on
the M2 in round 3 (`v3-repetition-outcome.md`). The three `lc1d-2` nets of that experiment
learnt a different rule and sit in an appendix table.

**TD(λ) under `lc1-2`, retrained.** Round 2's settings (`v2-td.md` § "Under `lc1-2`"), three
seeds, 1,000 iterations of 50 games each, then the value net exported for the pure-Rust
bench. The commands, as in `scripts/v4-eval.sh` and each run's `config.json`:

```bash
L=$(find target/release/build -path "*/torch-sys-*/out/libtorch/libtorch/lib" | head -1)
for s in 1 2 3; do
  DYLD_LIBRARY_PATH=$L ./target/release/td_train models/v4-td-lc1-s$s --rules lc1-2 \
    --features mover-relative-repetition --lambda 0.7 --lr 0.1 --iterations 1000 \
    --temp-decay 1000 --eval-every 50 --eval-pairs 200 --checkpoint-every 50 --seed $s
  DYLD_LIBRARY_PATH=$L ./target/release/export_td models/v4-td-lc1-s$s/weights.pt \
    models/v4-td-lc1-s$s/td.pfv
done
```

**One mismatch, stated once.** The keep rates are read in the uncapped game's table, while
the nets, TD and the tabular learner learnt under `lc1-2` and the arena plays `lc1-2`. The
table says nothing about repetition. Each figure this concerns carries a † in the tables and
in the text below.

**Controls.** Before any player is measured, on the full held-out sample: the table oracle
must keep 1.000 / 1.000; its complement (every move that does not keep the value, or every
move when all keep it) must equal the share of positions where every move keeps, 0.008 /
0.000 in round 3; and `random` must reproduce round 3's 0.360 / 0.112 to the third decimal.
A failed control exits 2 and writes no JSON. Every committed JSON records them under
`controls`: oracle 1.0 / 1.0, complement 0.0077 / 0.0001, random 0.35965 / 0.11199.

Each command of the tables runs with 4 threads (`threads` in each JSON) on the 8 GB M2.

## Pilot

`v4bench` on the bundled net, sample cut to 500 positions per class and 50 arena pairs
(the controls still run on the full 10,000 + 10,000 sample):

```bash
/usr/bin/time -l ./target/release/v4bench models/v3-solve net:crates/cli/assets/az-lc1-s1.pfw \
  --sample-cap 500 --pairs 50 --out pilot.json
```

Measured on the 8 GB M2 Mac, 4 threads: wall time 6.71 s; max RSS from `/usr/bin/time -l`
585 MB. Phases (the binary's own timings): table SHA-256 1.38 s, load 0.11 s, sample
0.67 s, controls 0.59 s, value kept on 1,000 positions 1.43 s, arena 2.27 s (50 pairs,
one thread).

Full run per net at 100 simulations (20,000 positions, 1,000 pairs), **extrapolated from
pilot**: 2.8 s fixed + 1.43 × 20 ≈ 29 s + 2.27 × 20 ≈ 45 s, about **77 s**, with max RSS
near 0.59 GB (the table dominates). The arena term is the noisy one: it rests on 50 pairs
whose game lengths vary with the opening, and a net that plays longer games takes longer.

TD(λ) retrain under `lc1-2`, 20-iteration pilot (seed 1, scratch directory, deleted):

```bash
/usr/bin/time -l env DYLD_LIBRARY_PATH=$L ./target/release/td_train models/v4-td-pilot \
  --rules lc1-2 --features mover-relative-repetition --lambda 0.7 --lr 0.1 --iterations 20 \
  --temp-decay 1000 --eval-every 50 --eval-pairs 200 --checkpoint-every 50 --seed 1
```

Measured: 2.69 s wall, 116 MB max RSS from `/usr/bin/time -l` (no evaluation fell inside 20 iterations). 1,000
iterations (50,000 games), **extrapolated from pilot**: about 135 s plus 20 evaluations. The
three full runs took 61.0 s, 61.7 s and 61.4 s each (evaluations included), 111–117 MB; the
pilot overestimated by about 2.2 times, because early iterations, at high temperature and
with an untrained net, play longer games than later ones.

## Tabular learner — pre-registration

Written and committed before any pilot or training run of the learner (Task 7 of the v4
plan). The criteria and the prediction below are fixed here; a failure stays failed, with no
retuning and no rerun.

**Design.**

- **One value per position, dense.** `Vec<f32>` indexed by `Game::rank` (`retro.rs`):
  232,792,560 × 4 B = 931 MB, plus a 29 MB visited bitset; fits the 8 GB M2. A sparse map's
  cost per visited position is measured during the pilot (a `HashMap<u64, f32>` filled to
  1 M entries, peak RSS read; estimate before measuring: ~40 B), which gives the visited count
  above which it passes 931 MB; the dense array is in any case O(1), deterministic and saved
  as one file. Stored as `models/v4-qtab-sN/v.bin` (gitignored).
- **This is V, not Q.** The game is deterministic: Q(s, a) = r(s, a) if the move ends the
  game, else −V(s′), where s′ is the position after a (the opponent to move). One value per
  position therefore defines Q for every move.
- **Update (Q-learning, afterstate form):** at each ply, before the move,
  V(s) ← V(s) + α·(max_a Q(s, a) − V(s)), with r = +1 if the move wins for the mover, −1 if it
  loses (including the third occurrence under `lc1-2`, read from `is_terminal` on the real
  history). Behaviour: ε-greedy on Q, ties uniform.
- **Constants, pre-set:** V₀ = 0, α = 0.1, ε decays linearly from 1.0 to 0.05 over the run,
  1,000-ply limit. A truncated game has no result: no terminal reward; the per-ply updates
  already made stand (they never needed the outcome).
- **Training under `lc1-2`.** V ignores history, so under `lc1-2` it is not a Markov state
  (same mismatch as the nets); the max target still sees repetition losses through the live
  rules.
- **Three seeds**, resumable (Ctrl+C stops at a game boundary; V, counters, generator saved),
  as `td_train`.
- **Budget:** a 5-minute pilot measures positions/s; the run length is set so one seed takes
  about 1 h extrapolated, written in this document labelled "extrapolated from pilot" before
  the run.

The bench plays the learnt table as `qtab:<v.bin>`: its tied set is the moves of highest
Q(s, a) as defined above (the exact result if the move ends the game under the live rules,
else −V(s′)), exact float ties only.

**Pre-registered criteria** (reported as met or failed; a failure stays failed, no retuning and
rerun):

1. Against `random` (1,000 pairs, seed 404): lower 95 % bound > 0.5 on all three seeds.
2. Against `greedy` (same protocol): lower 95 % bound > 0.5 on all three seeds — the bar TD
   met in round 2.
3. Value kept on the common held-out sample above random's, lower bound > 0.360 (won) and
   > 0.112 (drawn), on all three seeds. **Written prediction:** close to random's rates,
   because most sample positions have no visited child; if so, this criterion measures
   coverage, and the table-to-TD gap measures what generalisation brings.
4. Reported without threshold: the same keep rate on the sample positions with at least one
   visited child (with n), and the share of reachable positions visited.

`random` in criterion 1 is the bench's `random` row (row 2 of the main table: every legal
move after taking a move that wins at once and avoiding one that loses at once, round 3's
`Policy::Random`), not the arena's uniform `Scripted::Random`. `greedy` in criterion 2 is the
bench's usual opponent.

### Tabular learner — pilot and budget

Five minutes of `qtrain` on seed 1 in a scratch directory (deleted), stopped by SIGINT:

With `--games 100000000`, ε stayed near 1.0 throughout (0.9996 at the end): the pilot measures
almost uniform play. Measured on the 8 GB M2 Mac, one thread: 42,502 games and 5,671,034 plies
(updates) in 300.6 s, so 141 games/s and 18,860 positions/s, 133 plies per game on average;

`/usr/bin/time -l` 997 MB. Five saves of `v.bin` + `visited.bin` fell inside the 300 s and are

Sparse map, measured once (a standalone program, `HashMap<u64, f32>` with the standard hasher,
1,000,000 random keys below 232,792,560, of which 997,780 distinct): peak RSS 73.2 MB against

made when the map grows). A map passes the dense array's 931 MB at about 13 M entries; the pilot
visited 5.4 M positions in 5 minutes.

**Budget, extrapolated from pilot:** `--games 500000` per seed, about 500,000 / 141 ≈ 3,540 s,
just under 1 h, and about 66 M updates. The extrapolation rests on near-uniform play; as ε
falls, the games' length can change (greedy play may repeat positions and end on a third
occurrence, or run to the limit), and the run time with it.

### Tabular learner — verdict

**Criterion 1 is met, criteria 2 and 3 failed, on all three seeds.** The learner beats random
head to head (0.540–0.548, lower bounds 0.519–0.527) and loses to greedy (0.043–0.045). Its
keep rates, 0.360† / 0.113† on every seed, do not clear random's 0.360 / 0.112 (lower bounds
0.3553–0.3556† won, 0.1116–0.1117† drawn). These failures stand: no constant was retuned and no run was repeated. The
runs were much shorter than the budget extrapolated from the pilot; the measured times and
the reason are under "runs and criteria" above.

**The prediction's outcome held; its stated reason did not.** The written prediction was that
the keep rate would be close to random's "because most sample positions have no visited
child". The rate is random's to the third decimal. But most sample positions *do* have a
visited child: 6,536 of the 10,000 won and 5,572 of the 10,000 drawn held-out positions on
seed 1, 6,488 / 5,517 on seed 2 and 6,515 / 5,537 on seed 3 (`keep_visited.won.n` and
`keep_visited.drawn.n` in `qtab-lc1-sN.json`), although only 6.87–6.88 % of the reachable
positions were visited. Coverage of the sample is therefore not what keeps the learner at
random's level, and this document does not claim it does. On those positions the learner
keeps 0.377† / 0.108† (seed 1, [0.371, 0.383] / [0.106, 0.110]). Random's rate on the same
subset was not measured, so these figures are not a like-for-like comparison with random's
0.360 / 0.112 over the whole sample, and they show neither that the learner does better than
random where it has values nor that it does worse.

## Decisions

Taken by the owner on 2026-11-08 before the runs, with their reasons. Criteria are numbered
as in the pre-registration above.

1. **TD(λ) retrained**, three seeds under `lc1-2`, exported to pure Rust and benched: it is
   the middle rung between the tabular learner (no generalisation, no search) and AlphaZero
   (a net and a search).
2. **Round 1's DQN set aside in writing** (above): its training protocol is invalidated by
   `v1-verdict.md`, the tabular learner replaces it as the search-free baseline, and measuring
   it would need a strict loader and an export for no reader.
3. **`tools/article-visuals/` versioned**, with `pogoq` attached to the workspace and a test
   (`pogoq values` on the centre opening reports `Win(11)` for the mover). No further reason
   is recorded.
4. **The README keeps round 2's figure** (0.855–0.887 against greedy, `v2-results.md`): it is
   still true and sourced, and this round measures one seed of that net (`az-lc1-s1`), so it
   cannot reproduce a three-seed claim.
5. **Tabular criterion 3 stays on the common sample**, with a prediction written before the
   pilot and a secondary measure on the positions with a visited child: restricting the
   criterion to visited positions would break "the same sample for every player".
6. **Tabular criterion 2, "beats greedy"**, was added: it is the bar TD met in round 2.
7. **`lc1d-2` nets in an appendix**: the main table holds only players trained under `lc1-2`,
   the scripted players and the perfect player.
8. **AlphaZero without search gets a row** (`net:…:0`): it isolates what search adds within
   AlphaZero.
9. **The rebuilt table is deleted once its hash matches** (decided by Claude, reversible):
   it is 466 MB on a small disk, and `scripts/v4-eval.sh` rebuilds it when absent.

## What we do not know

- **Why the tabular learner plays like random** where most sample positions have a visited
  child. The prediction's reason is refuted; no replacement is tested. How often each
  position was updated, and random's keep rate on the visited subset, were not measured.
- **How the players keep the value in play.** The sample is uniform over reachable positions
  and has no history; round 3 showed such positions are not the ones games visit. No keep
  rate here is measured on positions met in play.
- **The distance to perfect `lc1-2` play.** `lc1-2` is not solved from the start
  (`v3-lc1-solve.md`); the perfect player here is perfect for the uncapped game.
- **Whether the table-to-TD gap is generalisation.** The representation, the learning rule
  and the number of games change together (above).
- **Why AlphaZero without search keeps the value as often as two-ply greedy yet scores far
  lower against greedy.**
- **Other search budgets, opponents and seeds.** Nets are measured at 0 and 100 simulations
  only, against greedy only, on one arena seed; round 2's AlphaZero nets of seeds 2 and 3
  are not measured.
